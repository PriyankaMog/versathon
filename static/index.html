Know what is happening with health in your neighbourhood.

OutbreakRadar helps residents, health workers and health authorities notice unusual patterns of illness early, so advice and help can reach people sooner.

Check your area Report symptoms An awareness and early-warning service. It does not diagnose illness. In an emergency, call 112 or your local emergency number.

From a single report to an explained alert

Every step is visible, so health officers can verify an alert instead of trusting a black box.

1

Report

Choose a symptom and severity. Your ward is found from your location, which is never shown publicly.

2

Detect

Each ward is compared with its own normal daily count, then checked for clusters within 500 m.

3

Explain

Every alert shows its numbers and a four-part score, so anyone can see why it fired.

4

Act and inform

Authorities investigate, escalate or close the alert, and publish advisories to the public.

Built for the whole community

One shared picture of local health, with a clear role for everyone.

Residents

See whether illness reports in your ward are normal or rising, read advisories from the health authority, and report symptoms using your current location.

Health workers

Verified health-worker reports carry more weight, so what you see on the ground reaches decision-makers quickly.

Health authorities

Get explained alerts, decide to investigate, escalate or close them, and publish advisories that residents can read straight away.

Simple steps that protect your family

General health information. It is not medical advice, so follow guidance from your local health authority.

Safe water and food

Drink boiled or treated water when advised. Keep food covered and eat freshly cooked meals.

Clean hands

Wash hands with soap before eating or cooking and after using the toilet.

Stop mosquito breeding

Empty containers that hold standing water at least once a week, and use nets or repellents.

Get medical care early for a high or lasting fever, severe or long-lasting diarrhoea, signs of dehydration, or trouble breathing. In an emergency, call 112 or your local emergency number.

Built to resist false and spam reports

A health warning system is only useful if people can trust it. These safeguards are in the product today.

Accounts and verification

Reporting requires an account. Health workers are treated as citizens until the authority verifies them.

Rate limits

Each account can file a limited number of reports per hour, and duplicate reports are rejected.

Independent-evidence check

If most reports come from one account, no alert fires and the system explains why.

Trust scores

One-sided reporting lowers an account's trust score automatically and flags it for review.

Authority moderation

Officers can reject a report, verify, suspend, reinstate or ban an account. Rejected reports stop counting.

Audit log

Every automatic flag and every officer decision is recorded with who, what and when.
